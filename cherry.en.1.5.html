Only those who have known darkness
can lead others out of it.
The brighter the light,
the deeper the shadow.

Why do those with a calling
often suffer in the first half of life?

At its core, the answer is simple:

Those who have never known darkness
cannot guide others out of it.

A calling is not a reward.
It is a contract.

To carry light,
one must first be shaped by shadow.

In the early years,
those with a calling often face three kinds of pain—
not as punishment,
but as preparation.

1. The pain of the body

The vessel must be strong enough.

The body becomes a vessel
for something greater.

To hold more awareness,
more energy,
more purpose—

it must be refined.

Early physical struggles
are not random.
They are part of the making.

2. The pain of the heart

A broken heart opens deeper sight.

Misunderstood.
Alone.
Overwhelmed.

These are not weaknesses.

They are the roots of
empathy,
clarity,
and the ability to see through pain.

What cannot be learned
must be felt.

A heart that has been broken
can recognize the fractures in others.

3. The pain of life

Breaking down to rebuild.

Failure.
Loss.
Isolation.
Starting over from nothing.

This is not the end—
it is the clearing of what no longer belongs.

Only what is real remains.

From this,
strength is born.

Those with a calling
are not simply blessed.

They are shaped,
tested,
and refined—

until they become
a way forward for others.

The deeper the wounds,
the greater the light they can carry.

Not chosen because they are strong—
but made strong
by what they endured.

Pain is not punishment.
It is preparation.

It shapes the soul
into something
that can hold light.

And once the inner sight opens,

you no longer search outside
for direction—

because you begin to hear
your own soul.

This is where the true journey begins.
